First, the honest part

You searched for a developer, not a designer. There's a real difference.

Most people use the words interchangeably, and most Philadelphia shops are happy to let them. But you probably searched for a developer for a reason. Maybe you have a build that's more complicated than a brochure site. Maybe the last agency handed you something that looked fine and loaded like a brick. Here's the actual distinction, and where we sit.

The designer

Decides how it looks and how you move through it

Layout, type, color, the path from landing on a page to filling out a form. Good design work is genuinely hard and it's the reason a site feels trustworthy in the first three seconds. What it isn't, on its own, is code.

The developer

Writes the code that makes it real

Markup, styles, scripts, the server, the database, the integrations with whatever you already use. This is what decides whether the site loads in half a second or four, whether it survives a traffic spike, and whether Google can read it properly.

The usual arrangement

One shop does one half and subcontracts the other

It works until it doesn't. The design gets approved, then a developer who wasn't in the room has to build it, and the things that were never discussed become your problem after launch. You end up paying twice to fix the seam.

How we work

Both halves, same people, no handoff

We design it and we build it. Nothing gets designed that can't be built fast, because the person drawing it is the person writing it. That's the whole reason our sites come in at 90+ PageSpeed instead of whatever the template happened to ship with.
